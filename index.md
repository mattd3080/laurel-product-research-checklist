# A practical product-research checklist

A product page can tell you what a brand wants you to notice. A useful comparison records what you can verify—and leaves room for what you still do not know.

Use this checklist when you already need a replacement or are comparing a few options. Start with the job the product must do, then check the exact model rather than relying on a brand's general reputation.

## 1. Define the purchase

- What do I need this product to do?
- Can I keep, repair, or maintain what I already own?
- What size, compatibility, comfort, or performance requirements are essential?
- What is my budget, including replacement parts and upkeep?

## 2. Record materials precisely

Save the manufacturer's materials list for the exact model. Separate the main material from coatings, liners, adhesives, handles, and accessories. If a detail is missing, write “not disclosed” instead of assuming it is absent.

For clothing, note the complete fiber percentages and any advertised finish. For cookware, distinguish the cooking surface from the core and exterior. For a filter, record the system and replacement-cartridge model numbers together.

## 3. Turn broad claims into answerable questions

| Marketing language | Useful follow-up |
| --- | --- |
| “Tested” | Who tested which model, for what, and when? Is the report available? |
| “Certified” | Which certifier, standard, and product listing support the claim? |
| “Free from” | Which named substance does the claim cover, and what evidence supports it? |
| “Natural” | What are the actual materials or ingredients? |
| “Long-lasting” | What warranty, care instructions, and replacement parts are available? |

Keep the scope of evidence intact. A result for one component or model does not automatically describe every product from the brand. A certification name alone does not tell you which claims it covers.

## 4. Compare ownership, not just the sticker price

Check cleaning instructions, consumables, replacement intervals, warranties, and return policies. Estimate the cost over the period you expect to use the item. Record assumptions—such as how often you will replace a cartridge—so you can revise the comparison later.

## 5. Build a shortlist with category context

[Laurel](https://ask-laurel.com/) is a product-discovery resource covering home, kitchen, clothing, and kids. Its category pages provide a starting point for researching options:

- [Cookware](https://ask-laurel.com/products/cookware): compare cooking surfaces, construction, care requirements, and compatibility with your stove.
- [Water](https://ask-laurel.com/products/water): record the exact system and cartridge, the specific claims you want to check, and ongoing replacement costs.
- [Athletic wear](https://ask-laurel.com/products/athletic-wear): compare fiber percentages, fit, care instructions, and any advertised finishes.

Use discovery resources to find candidates, then return to the manufacturer's documentation and any relevant certification listing for model-specific details.

## Before you buy

Ask one final question: **What unresolved detail could change my decision?** If it matters, contact the manufacturer or choose an option with clearer documentation. If it does not, record the uncertainty and make the comparison on the factors that matter to you.
